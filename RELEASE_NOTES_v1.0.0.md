## Final validated project release

This repository presents a geospatial planning assessment of urban green infrastructure, ecosystem-service capacity, population-based demand, deficit and planning priority across Tartu, Estonia.

### Final headline results

- Reporting area: **38.8872 km²**
- Built-up land: **21.6040 km² (55.56%)**
- Dense Urban Forest: **7.5759 km² (19.48%)**
- Healthy Green Space: **6.2446 km² (16.06%)**
- Urban Woodland: **2.4494 km² (6.30%)**
- Mean ecosystem-service capacity: **41.54/100**
- Mean ecosystem-service demand: **0.5768**
- Mean ecosystem-service deficit: **0.4110**
- Built-up land in High or Very High planning priority: **72.30%**
- Deficit–Priority Spearman rho: **0.9802**

### Interpretation

The results identify a spatial mismatch between strong ecological assets and areas of concentrated urban demand. Existing high-capacity forest and woodland should be protected, while high-priority built-up areas provide the strongest locations for targeted green-infrastructure enhancement and nature-based interventions.

The priority surface is intended for strategic screening and should be combined with site-level information on ownership, access, engineering constraints, cost, community needs and field conditions before implementation.
