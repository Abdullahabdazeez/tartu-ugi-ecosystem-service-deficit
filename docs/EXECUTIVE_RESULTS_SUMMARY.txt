EXECUTIVE RESULTS SUMMARY

The final reporting area covers 38.8872 km² in Tartu, Estonia. Built-up land is the dominant class, occupying 21.6040 km² or 55.56% of the study area. Dense Urban Forest covers 7.5759 km² (19.48%), Healthy Green Space 6.2446 km² (16.06%) and Urban Woodland 2.4494 km² (6.30%).

Mean ecosystem-service capacity is 41.54/100, mean demand is 0.5768 and mean deficit is 0.4110. Built-up areas have mean capacity of 8.00/100, mean demand of 0.7339 and mean deficit of 0.6751.

The main planning result is the concentration of high intervention priority within the urban fabric. Approximately 72.30% of built-up land falls within High or Very High planning-priority classes, compared with only 0.02% of non-built land.

The results identify a clear spatial mismatch between ecological capacity and urban demand. Existing high-capacity forest and woodland should be protected, while built-up deficit hotspots provide the strongest locations for targeted green-infrastructure enhancement and nature-based interventions.
