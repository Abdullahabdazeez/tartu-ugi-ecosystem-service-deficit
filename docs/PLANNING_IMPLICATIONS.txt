PLANNING IMPLICATIONS

1. Enhance Green Infrastructure in High-Demand Built-up Areas

High-priority built-up areas should be assessed for opportunities to introduce additional vegetation and ecosystem-service functions. Where large parks are not feasible, smaller distributed interventions can still improve environmental performance. Suitable measures include street-tree planting, pocket parks, vegetated courtyards, green roofs, green walls and conversion of underused hard surfaces to planted or permeable areas.

2. Protect Existing High-Capacity Green Assets

Dense Urban Forest and Urban Woodland show the strongest ecosystem-service capacity and very low deficit. These areas should be treated as strategic ecological assets. Planning decisions should minimise unnecessary fragmentation, protect mature vegetation and maintain connections between larger green areas and the surrounding urban fabric.

3. Improve Green-Space Connectivity and Access

Where high-demand neighbourhoods are close to existing green assets, planning should consider stronger pedestrian and green-network connections. Improved walking links, tree-lined routes and connected open-space corridors can extend the functional value of existing green infrastructure without requiring entirely new large green spaces.

4. Integrate Green Infrastructure with Stormwater and Climate Adaptation

Bioswales, rain gardens, permeable surfaces, tree pits and vegetated drainage corridors can support stormwater management while increasing local ecological capacity. Priority areas identified in this study provide a logical starting point for locating such multifunctional interventions.

5. Use the Priority Map as a Screening Tool

The city-wide priority layer should guide where more detailed planning investigation begins. Before implementation, individual sites should be assessed for land ownership, public access, existing land use, engineering constraints, cost, community needs and field conditions. The geospatial model narrows the search area; site planning determines the appropriate intervention.
